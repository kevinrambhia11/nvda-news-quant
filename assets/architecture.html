NVDA news-quant desk · system reference · 2026-07-28

System reference

A daily pipeline that reads news and price data for NVDA and outputs a position size with risk limits. It runs unattended on one machine, produces recommendations only, and never places trades. Three models are trained and reported separately, and they are relied on to different degrees.

Outputs

The pipeline answers three questions each evening. Status labels below are used consistently throughout this page.

Expected volatility

SIZES POSITION

Forecast size of tomorrow's move, either direction. Converted into a position weight and one-day loss limits. The only output used to size risk.

Probability of a large move

UNDER EVALUATION

Calibrated probability that the next move exceeds its own recent median. The one place news measurably contributes. Published daily and graded after the fact.

Direction

ADVISORY

Probability of an up move. Nine model designs failed to beat holding the stock, so this is printed as context and not used for sizing.

The six article categories are NVIDIA, competitors, hyperscalers, AI companies, macro (tariffs, sanctions, central banks) and brokers. All sources are free. Loaders are cache-first and fall back to labeled stale data rather than failing.

Feature construction

Each row represents one trading day d. Features may use news published through calendar day d−1 and prices through the previous close. The prediction target is the open(d) to open(d+1) return. Roughly 53 features per row: price and technical, realized volatility, earnings events, market regime, cross-name news, and news-derived features.

The same code builds historical training rows and the live row, so a feature that could see the future in testing would have to see the future in production, which is not possible. This makes lookahead structurally excluded rather than a matter of discipline.

News representation

Headlines are encoded by MiniLM, a frozen pre-trained sentence encoder, into 384-dimensional vectors. Similar meanings map to nearby vectors, which allows headlines to be compared numerically. Two layers read those vectors.

Ridge impact scorers

TRAINED LOCALLY

Map each article's vector to expected next-day signed and absolute return. Aggregated per day into 11 features: impact-weighted tone, disagreement between same-day articles, novelty against the trailing month, and per-category volume anomalies.

NewsNet

TRAINED LOCALLY

An attention network. For each article it computes an importance score, then normalizes all scores to sum to 1 across the day, and blends the articles into one day-level vector. Yields 5 features plus per-source impact estimates. Trained on 1,923 labeled days; stopped at epoch 9 by early stopping.

Decay layer

TRAINED LOCALLY

Carries the above forward as exponentially-weighted averages at 3 and 10 day half-lives, so news influence fades instead of expiring after one day. Adds 10 features.

Attention determines how conflicting same-day articles are handled: rather than averaging them, the network allocates a fixed weight budget across them, and the residual disagreement is recorded as its own feature. Note that the models read URL slugs, not full article text.

Position weight = min(1, 0.30 / forecast annualized volatility). Value at risk uses a normal approximation on total volatility including the overnight gap, and is therefore a floor rather than a worst case. Earnings nights receive an additional gap-variance adjustment.

  • News scorers train on the scorer-training era only. Candidates are compared on the clean judging window (2021-12 to 2023-08), which those scorers never saw, so a model that memorized its own training era cannot win on that basis.
  • The crowned model is then reported on the holdout (2023-09 onward), which is not used for selection. All boundaries are fixed calendar dates, so weekly retraining cannot shift them.
  • Reports print Sharpe standard errors. Over the judging window that error is approximately ±0.77, which is larger than most differences between candidates.
  • Archived daily forecasts are graded after the fact. Because they are timestamped before the market opens and cannot be revised, this is the only evaluation unaffected by repeated reading of the holdout.

Operation

every 30 min
Headline logger archives timestamped headlines from all verticals. Builds a news history for later research; does not feed current models.
weekdays 17:00
Article top-up, then prior forecasts are graded against realized outcomes, then the trained models are applied to current news, then the output is written and published. No training occurs.
Fridays 15:00
News layers retrained on all labeled history and both tournaments re-run; deployed models refitted. Finishes roughly an hour before that evening's signal. Any stage failing aborts publication.
2026-08-01
One-shot database backfill to migrate the industry news series off a rate-limited source, retried weekly until it completes.

All times India Standard Time; the US market opens at 19:00 IST during daylight-saving months. Model files are written atomically, so an overrunning retrain cannot leave a partially written model for the evening signal to load.

Limitations

  • No demonstrated advantage over buy-and-hold. On the holdout, volatility-sized long returns 44.6% annually at Sharpe 1.29, against 63.7% and 1.26 for holding the stock. The risk-adjusted difference is smaller than its standard error and costs 19 percentage points of return. Drawdown is shallower (−30% vs −43%), but a static partial position would achieve much of that without turnover.
  • Single stock, single regime. All results come from NVDA across one AI-driven bull market, on a ticker selected because it became that market's central name. The drawdown benefit has not been observed in a regime where volatility rises without a subsequent recovery.
  • The holdout has been read roughly forty times across the project, and decisions followed those readings. Both surviving positive results are of a size that repeated testing can produce by chance, so recent findings are exploratory.
  • The volatility model is not tested against its strongest baseline. A zero-parameter smoothing formula beats it on the project's own selection metric at both horizons, but analytic baselines cannot win the seat. The model also forecasts intraday range while sizing requires total risk including overnight gaps.
  • Practical gaps. No account size is configured, so risk figures are quoted per million. No numeric criterion defines when to stop trusting the system. Indian tax treatment of frequent US-equity turnover, currency conversion and idle-cash drag are unmodeled and could exceed the measured edge. Two hours pass between signal and market open with no defined protocol for news arriving in between. One evening's signal has already failed without alerting anyone.
  • News data is thinner than the headline count suggests. About 58% of the archive is unique, English-leaning, multi-word text; the models read URL slugs rather than article bodies, and roughly two-thirds of NVIDIA-category slugs contain no NVIDIA-related term. The measured news effect may partly reflect attention and news-flow patterns rather than the content of specific stories.

Glossary

Risk and return

VolatilityHow much a price moves, regardless of direction; quoted per day or annualized.
AnnualizedA daily figure scaled to a yearly rate. A 2% daily move is roughly 32% annualized.
Target-vol sizingHolding less when forecast volatility is high and more when it is low, so risk stays approximately constant.
Value at risk (VaR)A loss threshold at a confidence level. A 95% VaR of $47,820 means the worst 1 day in 20 loses at least that.
DrawdownPeak-to-trough decline in account value.
Sharpe ratioReturn divided by volatility. Measured with error: roughly ±0.6 over three years.
Buy-and-holdOwning the stock and doing nothing. The benchmark any strategy must beat.
ExposureFraction of capital, or share of days, actually invested.
Open-to-open returnChange from one session's opening price to the next; what an evening signal can actually capture.
Basis pointOne hundredth of a percent. The backtest charges 5 per unit of position change.

Models

FeatureOne input number describing a day. Models see features, not the world.
Training vs inferenceTraining adjusts internal parameters from examples; inference applies a fixed model to new data. This system trains weekly and infers daily.
HARVolatility model blending 1, 5 and 22-day realized volatility. A standard approach that often beats more complex alternatives.
Gradient-boosted treesMany small decision trees, each correcting the previous one's errors. Used for the direction and magnitude heads.
Ridge regressionLinear regression penalizing large coefficients, which keeps it stable with numerous noisy inputs.
EmbeddingA vector representing text meaning; here 384 numbers per headline. Similar meanings produce nearby vectors.
AttentionAssigning each item in a group a weight, with weights summing to 1. Determines which of a day's articles the network relies on.
Epoch, early stoppingAn epoch is one pass over the training data; early stopping halts training when held-back performance stops improving.
Calibration (isotonic)Adjusting probabilities so a stated 60% occurs about 60% of the time, order-preserving.
AUCRanking accuracy, 0.5 is chance and 1.0 perfect. The magnitude head reaches 0.568; the direction model 0.491.
Brier scoreMean squared error of probabilities; lower is better and 0.25 corresponds to guessing.
QLIKE, R²QLIKE scores volatility forecasts, lower better. R² is the share of variation explained.
Half-life, EWMAAn exponentially-weighted average in which a value retains half its weight after the stated interval.

Testing

Walk-forwardTrain, predict the next period blindly, step forward, retrain. Prevents any prediction from seeing its own answer.
Selection windowHistory used to choose between models. Scores here are optimistic because the winner was chosen for them.
HoldoutHistory reserved from all decisions, so the winner's score is unbiased. Its value degrades each time it is examined.
Nested splitTraining news scorers on an early slice only, so the judging window is genuinely unseen by them.
Lookahead / leakageA model accidentally using information from after the moment it predicts. The most common source of misleading backtests.
MultiplicityTesting many ideas causes some to appear successful by chance; results must be discounted accordingly.
Standard errorUncertainty around a measurement. Differences smaller than the error bars are not meaningful.
Winner's curseA contest winner's score is inflated by luck, so subsequent performance typically disappoints.

News data

GDELTA public project monitoring worldwide news and publishing machine-readable summaries.
SlugThe readable tail of a web address, usually containing the headline. What the models actually read.
ToneAn automated positivity score for coverage language; useful in aggregate, unreliable per article.
CategoryOne of six news buckets, tracked separately because each relates to the stock differently.
SyndicationThe same story republished by many outlets, inflating apparent volume without adding information.
NoveltyDistance between today's news and the trailing month's average, measured in embedding space.
ConflictDegree to which same-day articles disagree in implied direction; recorded as a feature rather than averaged away.
